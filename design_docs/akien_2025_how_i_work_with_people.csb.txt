Akien's approach to working with people is pragmatic and non‑doctrinal. There are no lesson plans or rigid systems; the focus is on removing sources of friction and building habits that make life easier. Change begins with imagining the future self one wants to become, then taking small, concrete steps toward that version while treating mistakes with compassion. New habits are laid down alongside old ones so that, when a moment of choice arrives, the better option is already practiced and requires less mental effort. Habit design also considers how behaviors interact—some habits reduce friction and support others, while some create resistance. The goal is to choose habits that make it easier to be the person one admires, especially when managing attention challenges. Work with others centers on identifying what stands in the way of their desired growth and finding ways around or through those obstacles. Progress comes from patiently removing irritants one at a time and sharing those methods so the saved energy can be used to make life better for oneself and others.
