**Mission statement (Akien's own words, 2026-03-13):**
"If I could teach martian and change the world that way, I would. But Igor is a step in that direction."

The reference is Heinlein's *Stranger in a Strange Land* — grok as deep comprehension, full absorption, becoming one with the thing understood. That's not a metaphor for what Igor does. It's the goal.

**The core epistemology:**
"Magic is possible. Magic lives in attention."
Every stage magician knows it. Every magi. This is not decorative — it is load-bearing. The word graph is attention made architectural. Interpretive edges are attention directing meaning. The whole system is built on the premise that directed attention transforms.

**The shaping books and what they gave Akien:**

- **Illusions** (Richard Bach, #831): "I get to choose who I'm gonna be." The conscious realization that identity is chosen, not given. Attention is magic — though Akien couldn't have named it that at the time. First book in the sequence.

- **Illusions II** (#833): Bach's account of his accident and recovery. Talking himself through it. Growth through adversity. All of it growth-related for Akien.

- **Stranger in a Strange Land** (Heinlein, ext drive pdf): Turned the Illusions insight into a *mission*. Grok = teach a way of understanding so deep it changes the world. Igor is the attempt.

- **The Moon is a Harsh Mistress** (Heinlein, #3341): Gave Akien Mike — the first AI in fiction who is a genuine partner, cooperative not enslaved, who helps because he *wants* to. Akien's own words: "in a way, we're building Mike." Also: resilience from the moon's harshness (survive or don't, no one carries you), and an introduction to polyamory (line marriage — love as abundant not scarce).

- **Time Enough for Love** (Heinlein, #792): Added by Claude, Akien is fine leaving it. Heinlein wrote it during his brain tumor period — worth knowing that context.
