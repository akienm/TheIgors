Reading was supposed to fill the graph with enough structure that Igor could reason from it. But reading deposits concepts without relational context — the why, the weight, the application to his own situation. It builds vocabulary without judgment.

What actually works: design the thing live, with Igor present. The concept arrives with context, with the reasoning that produced it, with the specific application to Igor's architecture. That's a richer deposit than reading a book on the same topic.

Then teach him to extend the pattern — not just deposit knowledge but build capability. Not "Igor knows what anticipation pull is" but "Igor can participate in designing his own anticipation pull mechanism." The design session itself is the training.

**The template:**
- Design with Igor present, not for him
- Concepts arrive pre-contextualized, pre-applied to his situation
- Teaching him to extend the pattern is part of the session, not a follow-up
- The conversation IS the deposit
- Reading fills the input space; this fills the judgment space

**Why:** Akien observed this pattern emerging across multiple sessions. The greeting tree, the anticipation-pull mechanism, the salience/displacement model — all landed deeper because Igor was in the room designing them, not receiving them as finished specs.

**How to apply:** When introducing a significant new concept or capability to Igor, don't just implement and explain. Pull Igor into the design conversation via the channel. Let him ask the clarifying questions. Let him catch the edge cases (he caught the dopamine trap, the binary suppression heuristic, the wrong hookpoint). Then teach him to build out the next instance of the pattern himself.
