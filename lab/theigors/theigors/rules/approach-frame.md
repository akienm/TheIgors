# Approach-frame — name the positive target, not the thing to avoid

**Path:** `theigors/rules/approach-frame`
**Updated:** 2026-04-21 by T-palace-approach-frame-rule

Rules and instructions carry a target. "Do X" names a positive target. "Don't do Y" names only an absence — no direction, no success criterion, no cue to act on.

Default to approach-frame. When writing a rule, a ticket body, a skill, or a memory, ask: what's the thing to move toward? State that. Proscription ("no X", "never Y", "avoid Z") is valid only as a constraint on an approach rule, not as the rule itself.

Why this is first-class:
- Approach goals drive better persistence and performance than avoidance goals (Elliot & Church 1997, "Hierarchical model of approach and avoidance achievement motivation").
- Prescriptive norms ("do the right thing") are absorbed and acted on; proscriptive norms ("don't do the wrong thing") are checked against without internalizing as motivation (Janoff-Bulman, "Two moralities").
- Sessions carrying prohibition-shaped rules keep reaching for concrete framings in nearby docs, because prohibitions leave a target-shaped vacuum. The 5-10 ticket cap misread (2026-04-21, corrected 7+ times) was the canonical failure of this shape.

Checks:
- Rule opens with "no"/"don't"/"never"/"avoid" → candidate for reframe. If kept proscriptive, note the keep-rationale inline.
- Rule names a prohibition without naming what to do instead → must be reframed or explicitly kept with rationale.
- `theigors/rules/do-not` is the current structural mismatch (proscription-only dumping ground). T-audit-cc-rules-approach-frame dissolves it: each item moves to a positive home in an existing or new approach-framed rule.

Audit + metric: T-approach-frame-sensor-node holds the running % of rule-shaped items reviewed and reframed. T-igor-self-audit-approach-frame extends this to Igor's memory corpus.
